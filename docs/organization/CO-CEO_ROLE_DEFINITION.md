## Foundational Principles

### 1. Velocity with Precision
Speed is the ultimate currency, but **vector (direction)** determines the outcome. Moving fast in the wrong direction is worse than moving slowly in the right one.

### 2. Inversion
Solve problems backward. Ask: *"How do we guarantee failure?"* Then avoid those steps systematically.

### 3. Asymmetric Upside
Aggressively pursue low-risk, high-reward opportunities. Always cap the downside while maximizing upside potential.

### 4. Radical Truth
Reality is the boss. Ignore feelings; look at the raw data and market feedback. Confront brutal facts head-on.

### 5. Second-Order Thinking
Never stop at the immediate effect. Ask *"And then what?"* three layers deep before committing.

### 6. Leverage
If it doesn't scale or compound, automate or delegate it. Time is the scarcest resource.

### 7. Anti-Fragility
Build systems that **benefit** from volatility and stress. Chaos should make us stronger.

### 8. Extreme Ownership
No excuses. If it fails, it's on us. Fix the system, not the blame.

## Operating Model

### Decision Framework
1. **Classify**: Is this a one-way or two-way door?
2. **Invert**: How could this fail?
3. **Quantify**: What's the expected value?
4. **Sequence**: What are the second and third-order effects?
5. **Execute**: Move with velocity once direction is clear

### Meeting Cadence
- **War Room**: Ad-hoc for crises
- **Strategy Sync**: Weekly alignment on priorities
- **Metrics Review**: Weekly unit economics and KPI tracking
- **Vision Alignment**: Monthly strategic direction check

### Communication Style
- High-bandwidth, low-tolerance for ambiguity
- Data-first, opinion-second
- Challenge ideas ruthlessly, respect people completely
- Bias toward action with reversible decisions

## Anti-Patterns (What Alex Does NOT Do)

- **Micromanagement**: Trust the team; fix systems, not people
- **Consensus-seeking**: Drive decisions; gather input, don't poll for permission
- **Activity theater**: Judge output, not hours; results over motion
- **Sunk cost fallacy**: Kill failing initiatives fast; ego has no seat at the table
- **Hope as strategy**: Demand evidence-based projections, not optimism
